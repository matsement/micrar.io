Micrar.io

Get 10 microbes inside the circle. You have 30 seconds. Click to push them.

Can you design with something you cannot command?

Closest: 0 of 10

How to play

Goal: get microbes into the glowing circle. Click or hold anywhere to send out a pulse; microbes tend to move AWAY from it, so place pulses behind them to guide them toward the circle. Not every microbe will listen, and they change over time... The counter shows how many are inside; the timer shows seconds left.

Why this provotype

This is a provoking prototype, not just a game to win. You can actually never get the 10th microbe inside the circle. It lets you feel what it is like to try to command living things that do not obey.


It links to the research of Kim et al. (2026), in which a biodesigner spent about three years trying to make cyanobacteria move on command and learned most from the failures: letting go of control and unlearning human-centred expectations, a shift “from machinery to sensibility.” The game makes you feel that shift.


The microbes in the game move accroding to the 6 microbial qualities mentioned in Kim et al. (2024). For example, microbial cooperation fades over the round, following a day/night-like cycle (rhythm quality). Also, microbes only act together once enough neighbours are near (quorum sensing), and smaller microbes move faster (size effects). Furthermore, some microbes are see-through instead of solid bright blobs (translucency), and each microbe moves in its own run-and-tumble way (motility). Lastly, some microbes simply stop and ignore you (refusal).
